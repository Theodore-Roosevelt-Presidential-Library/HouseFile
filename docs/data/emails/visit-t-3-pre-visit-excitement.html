A few things to know before you step into the country that made a president.

YOUR ADVENTURE STARTS IN FIVE DAYS

In three days, you'll stand where a young Theodore Roosevelt rebuilt himself — on the buttes above the Little Missouri, where the prairie runs clear to the horizon and the Library rises out of the land like it grew there. Here's how to make the most of the day ahead.

📍 Theodore Roosevelt Presidential Library
3410 Chateau Road, Medora, North Dakota. Get directions →


GIVE YOURSELF THE DAY

This isn't a quick stop. Most people spend around three and a half hours moving through the galleries, then another hour outside on the nearly mile-long boardwalk and the grass-covered roof, with its walking paths and wide-open views. There's a spot to pause for a bite at Salt+Scoria when you need it, too. Add it all up and you're looking at the better part of a day — so come unhurried and let the place unfold. Your ticket carries your entry time; you can always double-check hours here.


DRESS FOR THE BADLANDS

The weather out here has a mind of its own. A few things make the day easier:

  • Comfortable shoes — there's ground to cover, inside and out
  • Sun protection and a layer for the wind
  • A lidded water bottle, the one drink that comes with you into the galleries

The campus is cashless, so a card or your phone is all you'll need to pay. And it's easiest to travel light — smaller bags come along, while backpacks and larger items tuck into storage on the way in. (A few more good-to-knows →)


FINDING YOUR WAY IN


The Library sits just above downtown Medora — park on site and walk up. Arrive a few minutes early and the walk from the lot and a quick security check won't eat into your entry window. If you need mobility access, there's accessible parking steps from the entrance, firm and level pathways throughout, and wheelchairs available at the Welcome desk. Anything you'd like arranged ahead of time, just reach out to hello@trlibrary.com — we're glad to help you plan.


WHAT'S WAITING INSIDE


This is where T.R.'s story stops being something you read and becomes something you walk into: immersive galleries, a recreation of his Elkhorn Ranch cabin, and an AI archive where you can hold a real conversation with Roosevelt himself. Step outside, and the boardwalk carries you into the very country that shaped him.



STAY A WHILE


You're in one of the most storied corners of the American West. Theodore Roosevelt National Park's South Unit, the shops of Medora, and the Medora Musical are all just minutes away if your day has room for more.


The country that made a president is waiting. See you in the Badlands.
